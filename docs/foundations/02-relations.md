## 2.0 Geometric Intuition

A relation is an arrow diagram: draw $x\to y$ exactly when $xRy$. Every named property is a shape-constraint on that diagram. Crucially, a relation *is* a set — Pairing + Separation already suffice; nothing new is axiomatically required, only vocabulary.

## 2.1 Signature

**Definition ($n$-ary relation).** $\mathrm{IsBinaryRelation}(R,S) :\equiv R\subseteq S\times S$; write $xRy :\equiv (x,y)\in R$.

**Definitions.** $\mathrm{dom}(R)$, $\mathrm{ran}(R)$, $\mathrm{field}(R):=\mathrm{dom}(R)\cup\mathrm{ran}(R)$ — exist by Replacement on projection formulas.

**Definition (converse/inverse).** $R^{-1} := \{(y,x):(x,y)\in R\}$.

**Definition (composition).** $S\circ R := \{(a,c)\in A\times C : \exists b\in B((a,b)\in R \wedge (b,c)\in S)\}$.

## 2.2 The Sixteen Named Properties (full predicate logic)

$$\mathrm{Reflexive}(R,S):\equiv\forall x\in S(xRx) \qquad \mathrm{Irreflexive}(R,S):\equiv\forall x\in S\,\neg(xRx)$$
$$\mathrm{Symmetric}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y\in S(xRy\to yRx)$$
$$\mathrm{Antisymmetric}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y\in S((xRy\wedge yRx)\to x=y)$$
$$\mathrm{Asymmetric}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y\in S(xRy\to\neg(yRx))$$
$$\mathrm{Transitive}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y,z\in S((xRy\wedge yRz)\to xRz)$$
$$\mathrm{Total}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y\in S(xRy\vee yRx) \qquad \mathrm{Trichotomous}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y\in S(\text{exactly one of }xRy,x=y,yRx)$$
$$\mathrm{Dense}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y\in S(xRy\to\exists z\in S(xRz\wedge zRy))$$
$$\mathrm{WellFounded}(R,S):\equiv\forall A(A\subseteq S\wedge A\neq\varnothing\to\exists m\in A\,\forall a\in A\,\neg(aRm))$$
$$\mathrm{RightEuclidean}(R,S):\equiv\forall x,y,z\in S((xRy\wedge xRz)\to yRz)$$

**Theorem (Satz 0.8, statements only).** Irreflexive+antisymmetric $\iff$ asymmetric; irreflexive+transitive $\to$ asymmetric; reflexive+right-Euclidean $\to$ symmetric; trichotomous $\to$ irreflexive+asymmetric.

## 2.3 Composite Relation Types

$$\mathrm{Preorder}(R,S):\equiv\mathrm{Reflexive}\wedge\mathrm{Transitive} \qquad \mathrm{PartialOrder}(R,S):\equiv\mathrm{Reflexive}\wedge\mathrm{Antisymmetric}\wedge\mathrm{Transitive}$$
$$\mathrm{StrictPartialOrder}(R,S):\equiv\mathrm{Irreflexive}\wedge\mathrm{Transitive}\quad\text{(automatically asymmetric)}$$
$$\mathrm{TotalOrder} := \mathrm{PartialOrder}\wedge\mathrm{Total} \qquad \mathrm{StrictTotalOrder} := \mathrm{StrictPartialOrder}\wedge\mathrm{Trichotomous}$$
$$\mathrm{WellOrder} := \mathrm{TotalOrder}\wedge\mathrm{WellFounded}$$

**Theorem (Satz 0.10, strict/non-strict correspondence, statement only).** $\mathrm{PartialOrder}(\le,S) \leftrightarrow \mathrm{StrictPartialOrder}(<,S)$ via $x<y:\equiv(x\le y\wedge x\neq y)$, $x\le y:\equiv(x<y\vee x=y)$, mutually inverse constructions.

## 2.4 De Morgan / Duality for Relations

Boolean duality applies verbatim (relations are sets). **New here:** the **converse operation** is a second, independent involution.

**Theorem.** $(R\cup R')^{-1}=R^{-1}\cup(R')^{-1}$; $(R\cap R')^{-1}=R^{-1}\cap(R')^{-1}$; $(S\circ R)^{-1}=R^{-1}\circ S^{-1}$ (order reversed — the "socks and shoes" shape).

**Property-duality table.** Reflexive, Symmetric, Antisymmetric, Transitive, Total are self-dual under $R\mapsto R^{-1}$. **Flagged, not self-dual:** WellFounded — $(\mathbb N,\le)$ is well-founded, $(\mathbb N,\ge)$ is not (no greatest element). This asymmetry is load-bearing for everything in [Part 5](05-order.md) (Order) below.

## 2.5 Indexed Families of Relations

$$\bigcup_{i\in I}R_i := \{(x,y): \exists i(xR_iy)\} \qquad \bigcap_{i\in I}R_i := \{(x,y):\forall i(xR_iy)\}$$

**Theorem.** Generalized De Morgan for relation families (direct instance of [Part 1.4](01-set.md)). Generalized converse duality: $(\bigcup R_i)^{-1}=\bigcup R_i^{-1}$, similarly for $\cap$.

**Theorem (flagged failure mode).** Intersection of equivalence relations is an equivalence relation; **union is NOT**, in general. Counterexample: $S=\{a,b,c\}$, $R_1$ identifies $a,b$; $R_2$ identifies $b,c$; $R_1\cup R_2$ contains $(a,b),(b,c)$ but not $(a,c)$ — transitivity fails. Resolved fully in [Part 3](03-equivalences.md) below (Equivalences, refinement lattice).

**Theorem.** Arbitrary intersection of partial orders extending a common relation $R_0$ is again a partial order extending $R_0$ — the mechanism behind "the order generated by $R_0$," parallel to $\omega$'s construction as the intersection of all inductive sets.
